## 2. Narrative Summary and Analysis
**Narrative Summary and Analysis**

During the reporting period from December 27, 2024, to December 26, 2025, a total of 1,024 adverse drug experience cases were reported for Bisoprolol, comprising 1,023 serious cases and 1 non-serious case. Within these cases, there were 1,023 total alerts (67 fatal and 956 non-fatal), with documented patient outcomes consisting of 68 deaths, 185 ongoing/not recovered, 203 recovering/resolving, 395 recovered/resolved, 14 recovered with sequelae, and 159 unknown. Observed data show that the most common adverse events were acute kidney injury (80 total/80 serious), drug ineffective (54 total/53 serious), hypotension (46 total/46 serious), drug interaction (43 total/43 serious), and dyspnoea (38 total/38 serious). Derived analysis of these top events indicates that acute kidney injury was the most frequently reported reaction overall and among serious cases, while drug ineffective was the only top reaction containing a non-serious case.

## 6. Trends and Important Observations
### Trends and Important Observations

During the reporting period from December 2024 through December 2025, a total of 1,024 adverse event cases were received for bisoprolol, representing an average monthly volume of 78.77 cases. 

The monthly case distribution remained relatively stable throughout the majority of the reporting period, with most months reporting between 75 and 85 cases. The key metrics and volume fluctuations observed during this period include:

*   **Trough Volume:** The minimum monthly case volume was recorded in December 2024, with 21 cases. Following this initial month, reporting volume increased and stabilized.
*   **Peak Volume:** Case reports peaked at 109 cases in July 2025. 
*   **Notable Fluctuations:** Following the July 2025 peak, a decrease to 64 cases was observed in August 2025. A secondary increase occurred in October 2025, with 102 cases reported, before volume returned to baseline levels in November 2025 (75 cases) and December 2025 (83 cases).

These monthly variations represent reporting volume fluctuations over the course of the surveillance period. These findings are presented purely as statistical observations of case throughput; no clinical significance or safety signals are inferred from these numerical patterns. Ongoing routine pharmacovigilance surveillance will continue to monitor these reporting trends.
